title: Water hardness and your coffee machine
description: Water hardness sets how often your machine asks to descale. Get it wrong and scale builds silently into error codes. Filters, descaling and fixes.

Somewhere in the first-time setup of almost every super-automatic, the machine asks you to set the water hardness, usually with a test strip from the box. Most people guess. That setting is not a formality — it is the metronome that decides how often the descale reminder appears, and when it is set wrong, limescale builds silently until it starts writing error codes.

## What the hardness setting actually does

Hardness is the amount of dissolved calcium and magnesium in your tap water. Heat that water to brewing temperature and a fraction of it precipitates out as limescale — on the thermoblock or boiler, inside the valves, along the tubes. Harder water means more scale per litre pumped.

Your machine cannot measure the scale as it forms, so it estimates. It counts the water it has pumped since the last descale and weights that against the hardness level you entered: set the machine to hard water and the reminder comes sooner; set it to soft and the machine waits longer. The setting changes the reminder frequency, not whether scale forms — that part is fixed by your water and your heating element.

The failure mode is obvious once you see it. If the setting says "soft" but your water is hard, the machine happily postpones the descale reminder while scale coats the heater week after week. The first symptom is rarely the reminder — it is a fault code.

## What limescale-driven error codes look like

Scale announces itself through codes that look like anything but scale:

- **De'Longhi "Ground too fine, adjust the mill"** reads like a grinder complaint, but on a machine that was working fine the grind has not changed — scale in the heater or infuser is the real cause, and a full descale is the fix for most cases on machines older than a year. See the [ground too fine page](https://uk.codefixcoffee.com/delonghi/magnifica-dinamica/ground-too-fine-adjust-mill/).
- **De'Longhi General Alarm** is the infuser nine times out of ten, but the next documented cause is scale making the pump labour; the message and its resets are on the [De'Longhi index](https://uk.codefixcoffee.com/delonghi/).
- **Pump faults**: a De'Longhi "Water Circuit Empty" that never primes, or a Philips pump that only wheezes after priming attempts, points to a scaled inlet or heater that will not let the pump build pressure.
- **Jura Error 6**, the ceramic-valve fault, has scale as its number-one cause: scale stiffens the valve disc until it stops reaching its commanded position. A full descale is the documented first step, and it is free — the valve codes sit on the [Jura index](https://uk.codefixcoffee.com/jura/). On the heating side, heavy scale slows heat-up enough to trip the thermoblock temperature checks on some firmware.

Not every blockage is limescale, and it pays to know the difference: [Philips Error 01](https://uk.codefixcoffee.com/philips-saeco/espresso-machines/error-01/) is the dry side of maintenance — ground coffee compacted in the funnel from oily beans — cleared with a spoon handle, not a descaler.

## Filter or descale?

Most brands offer an inline tank filter, and Philips ships AquaClean on its machines. A filter conditions the water entering the circuit and slows scale formation — but it does not dissolve what has already coated the heater, so it adjusts the schedule rather than abolishing it. Two rules from the service notes matter more than the brand:

- **The machine must be told a filter is fitted.** De'Longhi machines leave the descale light on when the water-filter setting does not match reality — a machine that thinks it is running unfiltered keeps reminding you like one.
- **A new filter must be primed.** A freshly fitted AquaClean that was never soaked traps air in the circuit, and the pump ends up drawing air instead of water.

## Setting and maintaining it properly

1. Test your water with the strip from the machine's box, or any aquarium or kit strip from a hardware store.
2. Enter the result in the machine's menu — every model keeps it near the descale or water settings; the manual names the exact path.
3. If you fit a filter, register it in the same menu so the descale counter and the filter setting agree.
4. Prime new filters as instructed — shake, soak upside down until no bubbles rise — before fitting.
5. When the descale reminder comes, run the cycle from the menu with the spout fitted and let both phases, including the rinse, finish uninterrupted. The machine only clears the reminder when it has seen the full cycle.
6. Re-test after moving house or changing your water supply, and reset the level.

The economics are one-sided. A bottle of descaler costs about €10. The things scale destroys are not cheap: a jammed Jura ceramic valve assembly runs €60 to €120, and scaled heaters and thermoblocks cost more than that again before labour. Five minutes with a test strip is the cheapest maintenance a coffee machine has.
