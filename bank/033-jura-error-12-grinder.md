title: "Jura Error 12: the stone in your beans"

Of all the codes a Jura can put on its display, Error 12 has the most literal cause: something is in your beans that was never supposed to be there. The control board commanded a grind and the grinder never turned. On two-grinder GIGA machines the code names the left grinder specifically. In practice the cause is nearly always a foreign object — classically a small stone picked up with the beans at harvest — jamming the burrs. The [full Error 12 reference](https://uk.codefixcoffee.com/jura/automatic-machines/error-12/) covers the detail; this post is about getting the obstruction out, and recognising when the fault is worse than a stone.

## What the code is telling you

Jura uses the same numbered errors across its E, ENA, S, J, Z and GIGA ranges, and Error 12 belongs to the grinder: the motor is seized or the burrs are impeded. Severity is rated medium and the repair is classed as moderate for DIY — harder than emptying a drip tray, but potentially achievable without opening the case, if the cause is a jam rather than a dead motor.

The GIGA detail matters. Those machines have two grinders, and Error 12 names the **left** one — so you know which grinder to investigate first rather than guessing.

## The usual culprit

Coffee is an agricultural product, and occasionally a small stone makes it through harvest and sorting and into a bag of beans. A stone small enough to pass the hopper outlet is exactly the right size to wedge between the burrs, and the grinder stops the moment it engages. Clumped roast debris — dark roasts sticking together in lumps — can impede the burrs the same way. In both cases the fault is mechanical, not electronic: the fix is to get the obstruction out, not to replace a board.

## The other cause: a motor seized on fines

The second documented cause is less dramatic but more expensive. Old, oily fines — the dusty particles that dark roasts shed — pack into the grinder over time, and the motor can eventually seize on them. The signature is telling: you have emptied the hopper, vacuumed the throat, and the grinder now **hums without turning**. The path is clear and the motor still cannot break away from rest, which means the motor is seized and needs replacement.

## The GIGA 6 exception

One caveat before you touch anything. On the GIGA 6, the same Error 12 can flag a communication or sensor fault rather than a jam, and a power cycle addresses that variant first. So on every Error 12, whatever the model, the first step is the cheapest one: unplug, wait, restart.

## Clearing it, step by step

1. Unplug the machine for five minutes, then restart. On a GIGA 6, this alone clears the communication-fault variant of the code.
2. Empty the bean hopper completely. Inspect the beans from the bottom of the hopper for stones or clumped roasts — if you find a stone, you have probably found the entire fault.
3. Vacuum the hopper outlet and the grinder throat with a narrow nozzle. Do not poke anything metal into the burrs; a scratched burr set is an avoidable expense.
4. Empty the grounds container and the drip tray, reinsert both, and restart so the machine can re-home itself.
5. Attempt a coffee. If the grinder turns, the jam is cleared. If it hums without turning now that the path is clear, the motor is seized and needs replacement.

## What it costs

- **Grinder motor:** €60 to €140 by model.
- **Burr set:** €25 to €45 if the burrs were damaged by the jam.
- **Bench repair:** out-of-warranty manufacturer service on a super-automatic typically runs €250 to €500 including return shipping; independent espresso repairers are usually cheaper for a single-part job.

Burr and grinder-motor replacement on a GIGA means significant teardown, so it is a bench repair unless you already service these machines. The repair is generally worth it on GIGA and Z machines; on something older and smaller, weigh the quote against the value of the machine.

## After the fix

Make a habit of looking at the beans before they go into the hopper — especially the first pour from a new bag or a different roaster. The one-sentence prevention for Error 12 is simply: check your beans for stones before refilling.
