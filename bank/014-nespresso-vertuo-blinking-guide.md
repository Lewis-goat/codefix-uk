Most Nespresso Vertuo machines have no display, so they speak in light. The Next, Plus, Pop and Evoluo line reports nearly everything — heating, descaling, faults — as blinking patterns, and the numeric 1300-family codes only surface on connected models. The good news: the language is small, it is documented on Nespresso's assistance pages, and almost every pattern decodes to one of four things. Our full [blinking-lights decoder](https://uk.codefixcoffee.com/nespresso/vertuo-machines/blinking-lights/) has the complete map; this is the field version.

## Step one: count before you touch anything

The whole diagnostic is colour, rhythm and count. Before unplugging or pressing anything, note three things:

- the colour of the light
- whether it is steady, blinking, or alternating in a sequence
- how many blinks there are per group, and whether the pattern repeats

Thirty seconds of watching tells you which family you are in — and stops you "fixing" a machine that is simply heating.

## Normal: the start-up sequence

Sequential or alternating blinks right after you power the machine on are heating mode. This is not a fault. Wait 15 to 25 seconds for the machine to reach temperature and the light will settle into its ready state. Only keep reading if the pattern never resolves.

## The orange family: descale

Orange, in any pattern, belongs to the descale system — the machine needs a descale, has one in progress, or considers one overdue.

1. Run the descale from the machine's descale mode with proper Nespresso descaler and a large container under the spout.
2. Let both phases — descaling and rinsing — finish uninterrupted. A cancelled descale is the classic way these machines get stuck in the mode.
3. Empty and refill the water tank afterwards, then run another rinse if the machine offers one.

Blinking orange that never ends almost always means a descale was started and never finished. The fix is not another reset; it is finishing the cycle.

One warning that matters: do not descale with vinegar. It damages the circuit and it voids the help Nespresso would otherwise give you. Proper descaler is about €10 to €15.

## Red: fault states

A steady or repeating red light is the machine reporting a genuine fault state — typically overheating or an internal error.

1. Unplug the machine for at least 10 minutes. If it was hot, let it cool fully.
2. Plug it back in and retry.

Red that returns immediately after a proper cool-down needs support. The practical outcome is decent here: Nespresso usually replaces faulty Vertuo machines under warranty rather than repairing them.

### On connected models: the 1301–1305 family

Connected Vertuo machines can also show numeric codes. Owner reports consistently tie 1301 to a machine stuck in — or needing — descale mode, and to the pod sensor not reading a capsule, with the neighbouring codes in the same family. Treat these as strong community signals rather than an official list; the fixes are the same sequences anyway. If the machine complains about a capsule that is clearly there, remove the capsule holder and wipe the capsule window and machine head so the sensor can read the pod barcode. The [1301–1305 page](https://uk.codefixcoffee.com/nespresso/vertuo-machines/1301-1305/) covers the full sequence.

## The 5-press factory reset: last resort, not first move

The official factory reset is worth knowing precisely because you should not reach for it first:

1. Put the handle in the UNLOCKED position.
2. Press the button 5 times within 3 seconds.
3. The light blinks orange five times to confirm the reset.

It is a last resort for two reasons. It wipes descale reminders and pairing, so you lose information you actually needed — and it fixes nothing mechanical. A machine blinking orange because a descale never finished will still be blinking orange after a reset, now with no reminder of why. Decode the pattern first, run the descale or the cool-down the pattern asks for, and keep the reset for a machine that is stuck with no pattern left to try.

## What it costs

Usually nothing: decoding the pattern is the whole fix most of the time. Budget €10 to €15 for descaler, and nothing else unless the machine needs support — in which case the [Nespresso section](https://uk.codefixcoffee.com/nespresso/) explains what to expect, including warranty exchanges.
