Samsung range and wall oven E-28: sensor short circuit

E-28 means: Oven temperature sensor short circuit (resistance too low).

Short answer. The sensor reads under about 930 ohms. A shorted probe or a pinched harness.
Part at faultoven temperature sensor
Severitymedium
DIY difficultyeasy
When it appearsany

How to fix E-28

  1. Cut power for 3 minutes.
  2. Remove and meter the sensor; replace if it reads low.
  3. Check the harness behind the oven for a pinch or burn.

When to stop and call a repairer

Cut power first.

What it costs

Parts: Sensor $20 to $40.

Service: A home visit from an appliance technician runs $120 to $250 for diagnosis plus the part. Boards are the expensive part; sensors and switches are cheap.

Worth repairing? Yes.

How to check the error log

Samsung ovens do not have a user-accessible error log. The code stays on the display until you fix the cause or cut power at the breaker for 3 minutes; if it returns after a reset, treat it as real.

Clear the log after a repair so you can tell whether the fault has really gone.

FAQ

What does E-28 mean on the Samsung range and wall oven?

Oven temperature sensor short circuit (resistance too low). The sensor reads under about 930 ohms. A shorted probe or a pinched harness.

Can I fix E-28 myself?

Is it worth repairing?

E-28 during startup or during use?

Samsung range and wall ovens report E-28 any. That timing is a useful clue when narrowing the cause.

Can I keep using the machine with E-28 showing?

Usually you can finish the drink you are making, but do not ignore it — the underlying fault rarely heals itself. Work through the cheap checks first.

Will E-28 clear on its own?

Often, yes. The first step (cut power for 3 minutes) clears a share of cases on its own. If the code comes straight back, the part is genuinely at fault.

How long does the repair take?

Fifteen to thirty minutes with basic tools, once you have the part.
