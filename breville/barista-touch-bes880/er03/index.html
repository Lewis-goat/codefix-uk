Sage / Breville Barista Touch ER03: temperature sensor short circuit

ER03 means: Ferro heater temperature sensor short circuit detected at startup.

Short answer. The ThermoJet (ferro) heater temperature sensor (NTC) is reading as a short circuit: the board sees near-zero resistance, which a healthy sensor never produces. The two real-world causes are water bridging the sensor contacts (a leak or steam at the probe seal) and a failed sensor. Because it failed at startup, it is usually a wet connector or a sensor that has already died.

How to fix ER03

  1. Switch off at the wall and unplug. Leave the machine for an hour so any moisture in the connectors dries, then try again. A short that clears after drying tells you there is a leak to find.
  2. Open the machine (base or rear panel) and inspect the ThermoJet (ferro) heater temperature sensor (NTC) plug and the wiring near the boiler. Look for a wet patch, white scale trails or a perished o-ring around the probe.
  3. Replace any leaking o-rings at the boiler fittings and NTC probes. Kits are cheap and the leak, not the sensor, is often the true fault.
  4. If the code persists after everything is dry, replace the ThermoJet (ferro) heater temperature sensor (NTC).
  5. Clear the error log and run the machine through a full heat-up and a shot to confirm.

When to stop and call a repairer

If you see scorch marks on the triac board or the machine trips your GFCI / RCD, stop: that is an electrical fault, not just a sensor.

What it costs

Parts: Genuine NTC sensor assemblies run about $25 to $95 depending on which sensor it is (steam wand and milk-jug sensors are the dear ones). O-ring kits are $10 to $20.

Service: Out-of-warranty Breville service quotes for internal faults are commonly $300 to $500, sometimes more, plus shipping. Independent espresso repair shops are usually cheaper for a sensor swap.

Worth repairing? Yes in most cases. Sensor plus o-ring kit is well under $100. If a leak has soaked the control board, get a quote before spending anything.

How to check the error log

Switch the machine off at the wall. Hold the front Power button, switch the wall power back on and release when the logo appears. Enter the service password 00000, then open Error Counter to see stored faults or Live Debug for live temperatures.

Clear the log after a repair so you can tell whether the fault has really gone.

ER03 during startup or during use?

Sage / Breville Barista Touchs report ER03 during the power-on self-test. That timing is a useful clue when narrowing the cause.

Can I keep using the machine with ER03 showing?

Usually you can finish the drink you are making, but do not ignore it — the underlying fault rarely heals itself. Work through the cheap checks first.

Will ER03 clear on its own?

Sometimes. Try the reset and cleaning steps first — they cost nothing and clear the milder cases. A code that returns within a day or two needs the parts fix.

How long does the repair take?

Thirty minutes to an hour if you are comfortable opening the case; budget an afternoon the first time.
