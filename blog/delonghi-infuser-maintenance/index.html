"De'Longhi infuser care: the monthly five-minute routine"

2026-10-11

On a De'Longhi Magnifica, Dinamica or PrimaDonna, the part doing the actual espresso work is the infuser: the brew unit that receives the grounds, presses them into a puck, pushes hot water through them and dumps the spent cake into the grounds container. Unlike a Jura, where the brew group lives permanently inside the machine, the De'Longhi infuser slides out through the service door without tools. That is an invitation — five minutes of care a month prevents most of the alarms these machines produce.

What the infuser actually does

Every cup follows the same cycle. The grinder drops fresh grounds into the infuser, the piston compresses them, the pump pushes water through the coffee under pressure, then the infuser opens and ejects the puck. Coffee oils and fine dust coat the mechanism every time. Left alone, they dry out, the moving parts stiffen, the piston slows, and the machine gradually loses track of where the infuser is. Most of the messages a De'Longhi shows trace back to that sticky mechanism.

The five-minute monthly routine

  1. Switch the machine off at the switch on the back and unplug it.
  2. Open the service door, press the two red release buttons and slide the infuser out. Take out the drip tray and grounds container first if they are in the way.
  3. Rinse the infuser thoroughly under lukewarm running water. Water only — no washing-up liquid, and never the dishwasher.
  4. Work the piston up and down by hand under the running water until it moves freely through its whole travel.
  5. Let the infuser air dry, then rub a thin film of food-safe silicone grease on the side guide rails and the piston seal. A tube costs €8 to €12 and lasts for years.
  6. Refit the infuser until it clicks, with its lever fully down, close the service door, switch on and run a rinse cycle.

Do not force anything at any point. The infuser is plastic; a cracked guide rail or a snapped tab means a replacement, and forcing it is how they crack.

What "Insert infuser assembly" really means

This message is not a fault report — it means the machine cannot detect the infuser in its seat, and it will refuse to run until it can. The usual causes: the infuser was refitted after cleaning but is not fully home, a stray puck or debris is blocking the rails, the lever is not pressed down, or the piston was not in its rest position before refitting. Press the top of the piston until it clicks before you slide the unit in.

The fix is to take it back out, check the cavity for debris with a torch and tweezers if needed, and refit it along the guides until it clicks firmly. If the message survives that, unplug for a minute and try again — and if it still returns, inspect the infuser for a broken tab. A replacement infuser runs €35 to €55 depending on model.

How this prevents General Alarm

General Alarm is De'Longhi's "something is wrong" message: the board detected a fault it cannot isolate. On Magnifica, Dinamica and PrimaDonna machines it is the infuser nine times out of ten — stuck, dirty, out of position, or carrying coffee dust on the small sensor window inside the cavity that reads its position. Newer models also log a numeric code, typically 1101 or 1512, which service technicians use, but the fault family is the same.

The monthly routine attacks all four causes at once, with one addition: while the door is open, wipe the sensor window in the cavity with a dry cloth. If General Alarm ever appears between cleanings, switch off for 30 seconds, remove and rinse the infuser, wipe that window, refit firmly and restart. If it still persists, run a descale — a scaled water circuit making the pump labour is the next most common trigger.

While the door is open

  • Leave the grounds container out for at least ten seconds before refitting it. The machine only resets its empty-counter when it sees the container physically removed, which is why the "empty grounds" message sometimes refuses to clear.
  • Check the beans at the bottom of the hopper for stones. A stone that reaches the burrs stalls the grinder, which newer models log as code 1454, grinder stuck.

What it costs

The routine itself is free after the first tube of grease. A failed infuser costs €35 to €55 (kits 7313251451 or 7313251441 by model), a descaler around €10 — both cheaper than the service visit either one spares you. The rest of the messages these machines show, including the hidden numeric codes, are in the De'Longhi error guide.
